# Longitudinal Ethnography Protocol

- Instrument version: `0.1.0-draft`
- Status: `DRAFT`
- Fieldwork authorization: none

## Purpose

This protocol defines a prospective methodology for studying how people live through and interpret changing human roles in AI-mediated sociotechnical systems. It contains no observations and authorizes no contact with participants.

## Temporal design

Longitudinal ethnography is the primary design. A claim of longitudinal engagement requires repeated, documented engagement across time under a versioned schedule and field-relationship plan. A one-off interview, retrospective account, or isolated observation must be labeled as such and must not silently be represented as longitudinal ethnography.

The minimum duration, cadence, number of engagements, site-selection approach, and comparative design remain unresolved. They must be fixed prospectively before authorized fieldwork.

## Analytic boundaries

Methodological records keep four layers distinct:

1. observation: what the researcher directly encountered within a stated scope;
2. participant account: what a participant communicated, without treating it as direct observation;
3. researcher interpretation: the researcher’s provisional interpretation;
4. analytic memo: a later analytic development linked to, but not substituted for, the underlying record.

Causal attribution to AI remains an analytic question. Change must not be labeled a “Singularity” merely because it occurs in an AI-mediated setting.

## Trajectories

The method must be capable of representing adoption, resistance, non-adoption, ambivalence, normalization, reversal, role renegotiation, and re-humanization. No direction is presumed.

## Reflexivity

Every methodological record must preserve researcher role and position, access conditions, changes in the field relationship, interpretive uncertainty, and methodological changes over time.

## Readiness boundary

No research involving people is authorized by this draft. Before execution, [`governance/fieldwork-readiness.json`](governance/fieldwork-readiness.json) must pass the repository gate and all applicable external requirements must independently be satisfied. A passing file is documentation of a project decision, not an institutional or legal approval.
